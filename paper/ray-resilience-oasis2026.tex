\begin{abstract}
RAY (Resilience Assistant for You) is a disaster-resilience GeoAI system
designed around a simple requirement: every public claim must stay within
the evidence and leave a record that can be checked. Hourly federal feeds
and event builders turn hazard, damage, vulnerability, population, and
imagery data into H3 products for three deep cases. A \emph{Steward Harness}
checks spatial outputs, records SHA-256 lineage, enforces separate claim and
distribution policies, and tracks whether evidence is retained,
re-derivable, or cited-only. Residents receive context and guidance rather
than parcel damage assessments; planners choose the trade-off between damage
and social vulnerability. Six open vision--language models were evaluated on
1{,}225 labelled samples per model, but their outputs remain evaluation
products and are excluded from operational claims. Three recorded failures
led to new publication, citation, and evidence-scope controls. The result is
an executable accountability design for autonomous geospatial workflows,
with explicit limits on coverage, disclosure, and evidence use.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large-language-model agents can now operate geographic information
systems with little human intervention
\cite{li2023autonomous,zhang2024geogpt}. In disaster response that
capability meets an unforgiving audience: an emergency manager acting on a
tile-level damage estimate, or a resident reading a sentence about their
own street, cannot audit a transformer. The prevailing answer is a
disclaimer, which locates accountability in the reader. RAY locates it in
the system. Following \citet{yang2026accountable}, we treat an agent's
claim as valid only when the \emph{outcome} passes executable spatial
checks, the \emph{process} is preserved and inspectable, and the claim is
\emph{institutionally} authorized --- the right actor asserting the right
thing at the right resolution --- and we add a fourth question raised by
third-party data: what can a \emph{reader} do to check this, and may the
evidence be kept at all?

This paper describes the deployed system\footnote{Live site, app, and
repository: \url{https://rayford295.github.io/ray-resilience/}} along the
four axes of the Track A rubric. \emph{Autonomy} (\S\ref{sec:autonomy}):
the pipelines write, run and check spatial analysis on their own, and the
places where a human sets a parameter are deliberate. \emph{Robustness and
generalizability} (\S\ref{sec:robust}): missing data, failing endpoints and
mismatched geographies are declared rather than smoothed, by one harness
that produced three cases in two states and two hazard types.
\emph{Social good alignment} (\S\ref{sec:social}): vulnerability, exposure
and priority are defined operationally, the trade-off is the user's, and
equity is enforced as rules about who may be told what. \emph{Innovation
and reflection} (\S\ref{sec:vlm}--\ref{sec:reflect}): model output treated
as governed evidence, three self-caught violations, and the limits of the
whole approach.

\section{Autonomy Under Constraint}
\label{sec:autonomy}

\textbf{What runs without a human.} The data plane is a set of GitHub
Actions pipelines. Four keyless federal connectors (USGS earthquakes, NWS
alerts, NHC tropical cyclones, and NIFC/WFIGS wildfires) refresh hourly and
write append-only snapshots plus a national watch product. Deep-case builders
ingest registered files, execute the spatial logic, validate the outputs, and
append audit rows without manual low-level cleaning. Table~\ref{tab:cases}
summarizes the committed products and the limits that travel with them. A
hashed dataset registry (134{,}272 files, $\sim$33\,GB) carries a label
crosswalk onto one severity scale. CDC's $-999$ sentinel becomes a counted
missing value, never a number. When the Hugging Face Milton pairs disagreed
with the Figshare table on 182 of 2{,}556 pairs, the builder used the table as
truth and recorded the discrepancy.

\textbf{Debugging as a first-class record.} Autonomous code fails. The
harness's answer is not a retry loop but fail-closed stages whose
failures are written down: an over-strict tract-join assertion during the
Eaton SVI build was rejected, logged, and corrected in a later run, and
the log keeps both rows. Every artifact carries a SHA-256 digest and an
audit row (agent, inputs, UTC time); every grid cell must carry an
uncertainty block or the build fails.

\textbf{Where the human is, on purpose.} The language-model agent, Ray,
answers questions; it does not choose which pipelines run. We declined an
LLM task planner deliberately: a model deciding which analyses execute
cannot be reconciled with a policy that decides what may be asserted. The
human-in-the-loop points are explicit. A user selects a role (resident or
planner), which changes what the agent may say. A planner sets the one
value-laden parameter the system computes with --- an inspection priority
$t\cdot\mathrm{damage} + (1-t)\cdot\mathrm{SVI}$ on a live slider, tiles
scored with partial data flagged, every adjustment audit-logged --- and can
draw an area and ask about it, receiving an answer that covers only the
evaluated tiles inside it and highlights the tiles it cited. A resident
enters an address and receives one of three answers: covered, outside the
evaluated areas, or \emph{not determined} when a layer could not be read.
The system will not claim an address is outside areas it failed to look
at.

\section{Robustness and Generalizability}
\label{sec:robust}

\textbf{The Steward Harness} sits between every pipeline stage, and
between the model and every sentence it emits. \emph{Outcome validity}:
CRS assertions, join integrity, sanity bounds, mandatory per-tile
uncertainty. \emph{Process validity}: SHA-256 lineage in an append-only
audit the app renders from any layer back to source snapshots; the
gateway's audit stores a point as its H3 cell and a question as a digest,
so a caller can prove their question produced a row without the log
retaining what a resident typed about their own home. \emph{Institutional
validity}: one YAML policy with two planes of ordered, first-match rules,
default deny, validated at load. The \emph{claim plane} scopes what may be
asserted by role, evidence tier, resolution and geography; the
\emph{distribution plane} scopes what a build may \emph{publish}, and CI
verifies the assembled site against it and fails the deploy on violation.
One \texttt{published\_events} list feeds both planes. \emph{Citation by
default}: every factual sentence cites an artifact identifier or is
refused; the exemption set is closed, so an unanticipated sentence form
costs a refusal, not an uncited claim. \emph{Verifiability}: each artifact
carries \texttt{retained} $>$ \texttt{re-derivable} $>$
\texttt{cited-only} with weakest-link aggregation and a \texttt{license}
attribute; where map-platform terms forbid retention, the harness attests
to the request plus a response digest, a record publishable because it
holds no content, and a property test proves no payload reaches the audit.

\textbf{Real-world complications, declared.} \emph{Missing data}: 42 of
295 Palisades images lack EXIF GPS, so the grid covers photographed
structures and says so; a source that fails leaves the watch badge
reporting \emph{mapped-of-total} and naming the source; a class with
$n{=}30$ is declared as lacking power. \emph{Endpoints}: an ArcGIS error
envelope fails the wildfire connector closed instead of reporting zero
fires; live adapters time out at 30--60\,s and record the timeout; a
model call that times out is a recorded \texttt{LLMUnavailable}, and a
model the serving stack cannot load is a declared failure, not a zero.
\emph{Spatial misalignment}: tract-level SVI on an H3 cell is a downscaling
approximation stated per feature; a census block straddling tiles is
wholly assigned to its centroid's tile, and no areal interpolation is
invented; population outside the evaluated tiles is reported as a total,
never mapped into cells the event has no evidence for; post-event Milton
imagery is season-cumulative (Debby, Helene, Milton) and every feature
carries that attribution caveat.

\textbf{Generalizability.} The same harness, policy and app produced the
Eaton Fire 2025 (CA, wildfire), Hurricane Milton 2024 (FL) and Hurricane
Ian 2022 (FL) cases; adding an event is a dossier directory and one entry
in \texttt{published\_events}, and the policy contains no geography. The
Tier-1 watch is nationwide by construction. Where evidence does not
generalize the system says so; Table~\ref{tab:cases} separates verified
severity from density-only coverage. The test suite is the robustness
evidence: 363 Python and 67 app tests in CI, including a cell-by-cell
policy-matrix sweep (every role
$\times$ tier $\times$ resolution), adversarial claim checks, connector
tests against real error envelopes, and an allowlist drift check that
fails on any hand edit. Judges can run it in two commands from the README.

\section{Social Good Alignment}
\label{sec:social}

\textbf{Operational definitions.} RAY uses established measures and
keeps the remaining choices visible (Table~\ref{tab:definitions}). Each
definition is a committed artifact with a hashed source snapshot, so a
reader can inspect both the measure and its limits.

\textbf{Equity as enforced rules.} Fairness here is not a metric but a set
of institutional constraints about who may be told what, each a named
rule. \emph{No parcel for anyone}: no role receives a parcel-level claim;
tile evidence never becomes a statement about a house. \emph{No damage
assessment for residents}: they receive exposure context and guidance,
not raw damage assessments of their own street, because a false ``not
significantly affected'' costs the reader more than a refusal.
\emph{Planner damage at Tier~3 only}: planners see tile-level damage where
Tier-3 cross-view evidence exists. Declared unknowns
render as prominently as findings; \emph{not determined} is a distinct
answer from \emph{outside}; and no rule authorizes facility claims, so
answers about hospitals and shelters default-deny rather than guess.

\textbf{Community perspectives, honestly.} The Milton truth labels are
human damage perception, not an instrument reading, and the resident mode
speaks in plain language with every qualifier attached. Beyond that we
claim little: no community co-designed these definitions, and no local
knowledge enters the pipeline yet. What the architecture offers is the
\emph{place} where negotiated definitions would go --- a declarative,
human-readable policy file in which a community's answer to ``who may be
told what'' is a rule, not a prompt --- and the record that would let a
community check whether the system kept its word.

\section{Model Output as Governed Evidence}
\label{sec:vlm}

The obvious next step for an imagery-rich system is to let a
vision--language model (VLM) grade damage. The harness turns that step
into a question: under what conditions may a model's guess become
evidence? Model output is a distinct class, \texttt{model\_derived}, that
carries its own measured accuracy or carries nothing; no claim-plane rule
admits it; the gateway's evidence store refuses to load it. We ported the
Damage Recognition prompts of RAPID~\cite{yang2026rapid} verbatim (Prompt~C
single image, five DINS classes; Prompt~B pre/post pair, three
human-perception classes), recorded a SHA-256 prompt digest in every
prediction, and served open weights with Ollama on a 24\,GB RTX~3090, so
the run is keyless and repeatable. Three cases: Palisades 2025 (RAPID
Dataset C2, 295 images, all graded); Milton 2024 (100 pairs per class,
seed 2026); Eaton 2025 (300 per class, the $n{=}30$ minority class fully
included; the post-event image only, declared; five classes collapsed
onto the set's three repairability classes). Every prediction is a record
with image sha256, truth, prediction, response digest, latency and model
digest; an unparseable answer is recorded as such, never coerced. A stage
aborts if more than 20\,\% of answers are off-schema, a metric leaves
$[0,1]$, fewer than 95\,\% of graded samples fall inside the event's
committed grid, or any product lacks an uncertainty block. We report
accuracy and RAPID's $\mathrm{NCSE}=\overline{|y-\hat{y}|}/(K{-}1)$.

\textbf{What the runs say.} Table~\ref{tab:vlm}: (i)~on a single
post-event image every open model beats the 7B reference and the best is
within two points of the paper's closed models, with no monotone size
effect --- gemma3:12b (0.546) is within a point of qwen3-vl:32b (0.554);
(ii)~on pre/post pairs no open model beats the 7B reference, and each
fails with a different fixed bias --- qwen3-vl:32b calls 258 of 300 pairs
Severe (Mild recall 0.01), gemma3:12b calls 262 Moderate, mistral 213 ---
so a vote across models would not be a fix; (iii)~Eaton's $n{=}30$ class
is weak for every model (recall 0.03--0.43) while the large classes sit
above 0.9; (iv)~the qwen3 family spends its time in hidden reasoning
before a 60-character answer, and on Milton 24 of qwen3-vl:8b's pairs hit
the client timeout, recorded as unanswered (8.3\,\%, under the bound).

\textbf{What the harness did with it.} the agreement grids sit beside the
human-labelled grids they are evaluated against, flagged
\texttt{model\_derived}; the publication planner ships them as an
evaluation; the claim plane has no rule that admits them; the gateway
skips them and records the exclusion. A reader can verify every number
from the records without a GPU. What no one can do --- and what the system
therefore does not do --- is turn a 0.93 into a sentence about a street.

\section{Reflection}
\label{sec:reflect}

\textbf{Three incidents changed the architecture.} Table~\ref{tab:incidents}
records the gap, the fix, and the check. The repeated lesson is practical: a
constraint written in one place and enforced in another is enforced nowhere.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Recorded failures and resulting controls.}
\label{tab:incidents}
\scriptsize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
\begin{tabularx}{\columnwidth}{@{}lY@{}}
\toprule
failure & change and verification \\
\midrule
Parcel-level DINS file reached the public site & added a distribution plane,
generated allowlist, and CI gate; public files fell from 30 to 16 and the
restricted URL returned 404 \\
Uncited reassurance passed the post-check & replaced the numeric heuristic
with citation-by-default; acceptance changed from 9/13 to 7/13 because the two

\textbf{Limitations.} The builders read a $\sim$33\,GB corpus on one
workstation, so third parties can verify the committed artifacts and
audit logs but not regenerate them. The VLM numbers are one pass on 4-bit
weights on a 24\,GB card; Milton and Eaton are seeded samples, and the
closed-model Milton reference used a different subset. The gateway runs
locally without a hosted endpoint, auth or rate limiting, so the public
demo ships without chat; the re-derivable regime has run only against a
stub. Three deep cases are not national analysis capability, and the app
says so.

\textbf{Ethics and the societal role of the agent.} The disclosure risk in
this domain is concrete: per-structure damage is a statement about a
family's home, and our first incident shows how easily it leaks without a
rule that names it. We therefore treat the resolution cap, the role
denials and the content-free audit not as features but as the conditions
under which an agent may speak to the public at all. The value judgements
--- which index counts as vulnerability, how damage trades against it ---
are handed to people, in a file they can read and a slider they can move,
and the system records that they did. Autonomous-GIS agents
\cite{li2023autonomous,zhang2024geogpt} and operational platforms such as
TDIS~\cite{tdis2026} show what such systems can compute; registries
(EM-DAT~\cite{delforge2025emdat}, GDACS~\cite{gdacs}) show what they can
record. What none carries, per row, is what resolution of claim the
evidence authorizes and whether a reader may keep it. Our position is
that an agent in public decision-making should be a steward of evidence
rather than an oracle: it should compute more than it is allowed to say,
say only what it can cite, and leave a record --- including of its own
failures --- that the public it serves can check.
